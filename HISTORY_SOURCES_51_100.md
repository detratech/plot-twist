## IDs 51–75

| ID | Example used in game | Research source | What the source supports |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 51 | Hollywood studio star system · 1930s–1950s | [Smithsonian Libraries: *Hollywood Hoopla*](https://www.si.edu/object/siris_sil_1072194) and [National Portrait Gallery: George Hurrell](https://npg.si.edu/about-us/press-release/national-portrait-gallery-presents_star-power) | Golden Age studios had dedicated publicity machinery and carefully staged, lit, posed star portraits that deliberately constructed glamour and public image. |
| 52 | Norman Rockwell’s family America · mid-20th century | [Smithsonian American Art Museum](https://americanart.si.edu/books/rockwell) and [Smithsonian: Four Freedoms](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/smithsonian-institution/norman-rockwells-four-freedoms-brought-ideals-america-life-180968033/) | Rockwell deliberately staged idealized narrative scenes and became famous for images associated with American family values and aspirational domestic life. The paintings can represent real virtues without being ordinary documentary footage of every household. |
| 53 | Facebook emotional-contagion experiment · 2012 | [PNAS / PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24889601/) | A 689,003-person experiment altered the positive/negative content appearing in News Feeds and found small corresponding changes in users’ later emotional expression. Feed selection can affect what users experience and express. |
| 54 | Charles and Diana’s short courtship · 1980–1981 | [PBS: *In Their Own Words — Princess Diana*](https://www.pbs.org/video/princess-diana-v57acx/) | The documentary records the commonly cited figure that Charles and Diana met only about 13 or 14 times before the proposal. The game uses this narrowly as an example of major commitment developing before extensive real-life compatibility testing, not as a one-cause explanation of their marriage. |
| 55 | Mars Climate Orbiter · 1999 | [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/mission/mars-climate-orbiter/) and [NASA/JPL mishap lesson](https://llis.nasa.gov/lesson/740) | Ground software supplied English-unit data where metric units were required; the interface assumption was not caught by review/testing and the spacecraft was lost. Serious teams can fail when a basic shared assumption stays implicit. |
| 56 | Japan’s karoshi / overwork crisis · late 20th century | [International Labour Organization report](https://www.ilo.org/sites/default/files/wcmsp5/groups/public/%40ed_norm/%40relconf/documents/meetingdocument/wcms_358295.pdf) | Japan formally recognized death from overwork as a major occupational/social problem and adopted national prevention measures after long-running concern over extreme working hours and work-related health harm. |
| 57 | Canada’s wartime income tax · 1917 | [Canada Revenue Agency: History of taxes](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/educational-programs/purpose-taxes.html) | The Income War Tax Act was introduced to finance the First World War, was described as temporary and reviewable after the war, was reviewed but retained, and in 1948 was no longer treated as temporary. |
| 58 | Lower East Side tenement life · late 1800s–early 1900s | [Tenement Museum: Lower East Side](https://www.tenement.org/explore/lower-east-side/) and [Tenement Museum: rent and boarders](https://www.tenement.org/blog/the-rent-is-due-a-history-of-rent-at-97-orchard-street/) | Families commonly crowded into very small apartments; boarders and pooled family wages were real survival strategies that reduced privacy while improving economic viability. |
| 59 | Johnson & Johnson’s Tylenol response · 1982 | [FDA oral history](https://www.fda.gov/media/88718/download) and [FDA legal-history milestone](https://www.fda.gov/about-fda/fda-history/milestones-us-food-and-drug-law) | After cyanide tampering, Johnson & Johnson/McNeil withdrew the product broadly, while the incident also drove tamper-resistant packaging rules and later federal anti-tampering law. Immediate protective action and later responsibility/system redesign were distinct stages. |
| 60 | B. F. Skinner and operant conditioning · 20th century | [American Psychological Association](https://www.apa.org/monitor/mar04/skinner) and [Harvard Psychology](https://psychology.fas.harvard.edu/people/b-f-skinner) | Skinner’s work established how consequences and reinforcement schedules shape future behaviour, including the special persistence created by intermittent reinforcement. |
| 61 | *Sesame Street* begins broadcasting · 1969 | [Smithsonian National Museum of American History](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/stories/can-you-tell-me-how-get-sesame-street) and [Smithsonian learning-kit record](https://www.si.edu/object/sesame-street-learning-kit%3Anmah_1803632) | *Sesame Street* deliberately used television’s entertainment techniques to teach preschool literacy, numeracy and social skills, especially to children with less access to preschool. A screen can be designed as a genuine teaching tool rather than being inherently empty entertainment. |
| 62 | FDR’s bank holiday · 1933 | [Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/bank-holiday-of-1933) | Roosevelt temporarily suspended banking transactions during a genuine systemic crisis, Congress passed emergency legislation, banks were examined and reopened in stages, and the extraordinary restriction had a defined operational purpose and route back toward normality. |
| 63 | Cuban Missile Crisis backchannel · 1962 | [U.S. Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/cuban-missile-crisis/1000) | Direct and indirect communications, including private/backchannel contact, helped Kennedy and Khrushchev clarify proposals while public military pressure continued. The crisis also exposed the danger of misunderstanding the other side’s intentions. |
| 64 | U.S.–China rapprochement · 1972 | [U.S. Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1969-1976/rapprochement-china) | Nixon’s visit and the Shanghai Communiqué materially improved contact and reduced hostility while the two governments explicitly retained major disagreements, including over Taiwan. Cooperation did not mean underlying differences disappeared. |
| 65 | Grace Kelly and Prince Rainier wedding · 1956 | [Prince’s Palace of Monaco](https://www.palais.mc/en/news/h-s-h-prince-albert-ii/event/2019/may/exhibition-at-the-palace-monaco-6-may-1955-the-story-of-a-meeting-3450.html) and [Monaco Tribune anniversary history](https://www.monaco-tribune.com/en/2024/04/68-years-ago-prince-rainier-iii-married-grace-kelly-in-the-wedding-of-the-century/) | The couple’s highly public royal wedding followed their first photographed meeting by less than a year and became a major international media event; contemporary retrospectives describe the live European broadcast reaching about 30 million viewers. |
| 66 | Subprime mortgage boom · 2000s | [Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/subprime-mortgage-crisis) and [Federal Reserve Bank of New York](https://www.newyorkfed.org/newsevents/speeches/2010/tra100625.html) | Many subprime mortgages used teaser-rate adjustable structures that looked manageable initially but exposed borrowers to payment shock and refinancing risk later. Monthly affordability can conceal the full obligation. |
| 67 | Lehman Brothers and liquidity · 2008 | [Federal Reserve Board testimony](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/testimony/bernanke20100420a.htm) | Fed/SEC stress tests identified serious liquidity deficiencies at Lehman before failure. Assets and capital claims did not remove the immediate danger created by inadequate liquid funding under stressed markets. |
| 68 | Anna Sorokin’s fake-heiress years · 2010s | [TIME on Sorokin’s conviction and sentencing](https://time.com/5560432/anna-delvey-sorokin-trial/) | Sorokin persuaded banks, hotels and people around New York that she was a wealthy German heiress, using the appearance of wealth and social status to obtain real access before the deception collapsed. |
| 69 | MC Hammer bankruptcy · 1996 | [Washington Post contemporary report](https://www.washingtonpost.com/archive/lifestyle/1997/08/26/its-bankruptcy-time/3cc1bc3f-4065-42fa-802c-8ea7bfc3b1ee/) and [New Yorker profile](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/1996/08/26/under-the-hammer-2) | Hammer earned extraordinary sums, maintained a huge entourage/payroll and lavish lifestyle, then filed for bankruptcy as income fell and obligations remained. High income does not protect against permanently expanding spending commitments. |
| 70 | Household financial buffers during COVID-19 · 2020 | [Federal Reserve: Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2020](https://www.federalreserve.gov/publications/2021-economic-well-being-of-us-households-in-2020-dealing-with-unexpected-expenses.htm) | The pandemic exposed major differences in emergency preparedness; laid-off workers were substantially less able to absorb even a $400 shock. Cash-like reserves preserve options when normal income is disrupted. |
| 71 | Installment buying in the 1920s | [Library of Congress: 1927 installment-plan study](https://www.loc.gov/item/27008285/) and [Smithsonian: consumer credit](https://americanhistory.si.edu/explore/exhibitions/american-enterprise/online/corporate-era/shopping) | Installment purchasing and consumer credit expanded sharply in the 1920s. Spreading payments changed how prices felt and enabled more durable-goods consumption while binding future income to multiple obligations. |
| 72 | Railroad overinvestment and the Panic of 1873 | [Smithsonian history of Jay Cooke / Northern Pacific](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/robber-baron-gamble-railroads-brought-down-bank-plunged-us-into-first-great-depression-panic-1873-180982877/) | Railroad building created genuinely valuable infrastructure while heavy speculative financing and overinvestment helped produce firm failures and a major financial panic. Productive debt can create both capacity and fragility. |
| 73 | Employee stock options and vesting | [Journal of Financial Economics: “The option to quit”](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304405X1730257X) | Broad-based employee stock options can reduce turnover before vesting, with turnover rising after the retention period. A valuable benefit can intentionally make leaving more costly without being worthless to the employee. |
| 74 | Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire · 1911 | [OSHA historical account](https://www.osha.gov/aboutosha/40-years/trianglefactoryfireaccount) and [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/places/triangle-shirtwaist-factory-brown-building.htm) | The legal garment business employed workers in dangerous conditions; 146 died, and locked/poorly designed exits materially worsened the disaster. A fashionable or legitimate product does not settle the moral quality of the conditions behind it. |
| 75 | Women Airforce Service Pilots · World War II | [U.S. Air Force history](https://www.af.mil/News/Features/Article/497548/womens-legacy-parallels-air-force-history/) and [National Women’s History Museum](https://www.womenshistory.org/exhibits/women-airforce-service-pilots-wasps-wwii) | More than a thousand women completed rigorous flight training and performed ferrying, test, training and other military aviation duties, demonstrating relevant capability despite earlier role exclusions. |
