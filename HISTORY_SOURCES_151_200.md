# Plot Twist Historical Example Source Ledger — IDs 151–200

This is an editorial/research record only. It is **not loaded by the game**.

Accuracy and exact analogy fit outrank fame. Official, academic, archival, court, and first-party historical sources are preferred. When research exposes a cleaner example or a popular retelling that is too neat, `history-reviewed.js` overrides the draft mapping.

## IDs 151–175

| ID | Example used in game | Research source | What the source supports |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 151 | Columbia Record Club negative-option model · 1960s onward | [Federal Trade Commission decision, 1967](https://www.ftc.gov/sites/default/files/documents/commission_decision_volumes/volume-72/ftcd-vol72july-december1967pages320-411.pdf) | Columbia offered a very cheap introductory bundle in exchange for future full-price purchase obligations and then used a negative-option system in which doing nothing could trigger shipment and billing. A bargain up front can coexist with meaningful future commitment and cancellation friction. |
| 152 | Enron employees and concentrated retirement savings · 2001 | [U.S. GAO: Private Pensions after Enron](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-02-480t) | GAO reported that roughly 63% of Enron 401(k) assets were company stock. Employees therefore exposed employment income and retirement savings to the same company-specific risk and suffered both job and retirement losses when Enron failed. |
| 153 | Stock-market crash · 1929 | [Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/stock-market-crash-of-1929) | The Dow fell almost 90% from its 1929 peak to the 1932 low and did not regain the 1929 high until 1954. Long-run recovery does not protect money that must be used during a severe drawdown. |
| 154 | American Airlines AAdvantage · 1981 | [American Airlines history](https://news.aa.com/centennial/our-stories/the-evolution-of-the-aadvantage/default.aspx) | American launched AAdvantage on May 1, 1981 to reward frequent flyers and strengthen loyalty. Loyalty rewards can have genuine value while also being intentionally designed to influence future purchasing behaviour. |
| 155 | Hoover Dam financing · 1930s–1987 | [U.S. Bureau of Reclamation](https://www.usbr.gov/lc/hooverdam/faqs/powerfaq.html) | Power revenue funded operations and repayment of the federal investment; construction completed and in service by 1937 was repaid from power revenues by May 31, 1987 apart from the flood-control allocation. Productive borrowing can build durable capacity while committing future revenue for decades. |
| 156 | Long-Term Capital Management · 1998 | [Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/ltcm-near-failure) | LTCM combined famous experts, sophisticated mathematical models and several years of spectacular returns with very high leverage; the 1998 market shock produced huge losses and a private-sector recapitalization. Visible brilliance and past wins did not reveal the full failure distribution. |
| 157 | 401(k) account versus investments | [U.S. Department of Labor: Savings Fitness](https://www.dol.gov/node/63384) | Retirement accounts can contain investments with very different risk profiles; stocks, bonds, mutual funds, cash-like holdings and other assets remain economically distinct even when held inside the same tax-advantaged wrapper. The account type and the investment choice answer different questions. |
| 158 | Save More Tomorrow · 1998 onward | [Thaler & Benartzi, *Journal of Political Economy*](https://ab-d.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/05/thaler2004.pdf) | Participants committed in advance to directing a portion of future pay increases into retirement saving. The intervention was designed around defaults, inertia and self-control rather than assuming important goals would reliably receive whatever money happened to remain. |
| 159 | California Gold Rush prices · 1849 | [Smithsonian American Experience](https://americanexperience.si.edu/historical-eras/expansion/pair-miners-union-diggings/) | Gold Rush wages and opportunities could be extraordinary while food, lodging and ordinary supplies also became extremely expensive. Headline income can be a poor measure of the real economic improvement after local costs are included. |
| 160 | Induced road demand · U.S. cities, 1983–2003 | [Duranton & Turner, *American Economic Review*](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.101.6.2616) | The authors found vehicle travel on interstate highways increased roughly proportionally with lane-kilometres. Added capacity was real, but users responded by consuming more of it; capacity without a boundary can be absorbed by expanding demand. |
| 161 | Apollo moon deadline · 1961–1969 | [NASA: Apollo retrospective analysis](https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/sp-4503-apollo.pdf) | Kennedy’s 1961 decision converted a longer-term lunar ambition into a concentrated national programme with an explicit end-of-decade deadline, enormous resources and a clear finish line. Exceptional sprint intensity becomes more defensible when the sprint has a defined edge. |
| 162 | Benjamin Franklin’s daily schedule · 18th century | [Library of Congress digitized autobiography](https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/public/gdcmassbookdig/autobiographyofb02fran/autobiographyofb02fran.pdf) | Franklin’s published schedule begins the morning with “What good shall I do this day?” before blocks of work and correspondence, and closes with a review question. Deliberately choosing direction before incoming demands is not a smartphone-era invention. |
| 163 | Newton’s plague years at Woolsthorpe · 1665–1667 | [The Newton Project](https://newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/his-life-and-work-at-a-glance) | Cambridge closures during plague sent Newton away from ordinary university life; during this period he developed major work in mathematics, optics and motion. Broad learning mattered, but long uninterrupted periods also created room for difficult ideas to develop. |
| 164 | Cambridge Analytica and Facebook data · exposed 2018 | [Federal Trade Commission](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2019/07/ftc-sues-cambridge-analytica-settles-former-ceo-app-developer) | The FTC alleged deceptive harvesting of personal information from tens of millions of Facebook users and friends for voter profiling and targeting. A service can offer real value while users poorly understand the downstream price paid in personal data. |
| 165 | Yellow journalism · 1890s | [Library of Congress: *The Evening World*](https://www.loc.gov/item/sn83030193/) | Intense Hearst–Pulitzer circulation competition encouraged papers to become more sensational, strident and loose with facts because emotionally gripping material attracted readers. Attention-maximizing media was shaping perceived reality long before recommendation algorithms. |
| 166 | Wright brothers’ wind-tunnel iteration · 1901–1903 | [Smithsonian National Air and Space Museum](https://airandspace.si.edu/explore/stories/researching-wright-way) | Disappointing glider results led the Wrights to test hundreds of wing shapes, revise accepted aerodynamic data and then validate the improved design in another glider before powered flight. Research became useful by meeting repeated real-world testing rather than replacing it. |
| 167 | Air Canada chatbot ruling · 2024 | [Moffatt v. Air Canada, 2024 BCCRT 149](https://www.canlii.org/en/bc/bccrt/doc/2024/2024bccrt149/2024bccrt149.html) | Air Canada’s chatbot supplied incorrect bereavement-fare information and the tribunal held the airline responsible for negligent misrepresentation. Delegating an answer to an automated tool did not delegate accountability for what the company represented to the customer. |
| 168 | *Mata v. Avianca* · 2023 | [Federal court sanctions order via CourtListener](https://www.courtlistener.com/docket/63107798/54/mata-v-avianca-inc/) | Lawyers filed fictitious judicial opinions and quotations generated by ChatGPT, then failed to verify the cited authorities before relying on them. Citation-shaped text is not evidence until the underlying source actually exists and supports the claim. |
| 169 | Facebook cross-cutting news study · 2015 | [Science, DOI 10.1126/science.aaa1160](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aaa1160) | In a large Facebook study, algorithmic ranking and users’ own clicking choices both reduced exposure to ideologically cross-cutting content; individual choices produced more of the narrowing in that study. Personal preference and personalization can reinforce each other rather than one system doing all the filtering. |
| 170 | Lincoln’s Cooper Union speech · 1860 | [National Park Service](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/abraham-lincoln-at-cooper-union.htm) | Lincoln built the speech around detailed research into how the Constitution’s framers had actually voted and acted, using historical evidence to challenge an opponent’s claim. A persuasive correction can be forceful without depending mainly on ridicule. |
| 171 | Bill Gates’ “Think Week” · 1990s onward | [Microsoft historical publication](https://download.microsoft.com/documents/UK/developingthefuture/Developing%20_The_Future_07.pdf) | Gates periodically withdrew from normal executive demands to read papers, think about long-term technologies and write responses. Useful input still requires protected processing time; filling every empty moment with more input can crowd out synthesis. |
| 172 | Adding friction to TikTok · 2026 experiment | [Aalto University research portal](https://research.aalto.fi/en/publications/designing-friction-for-digital-wellbeing-an-experimental-study-of/) | Researchers experimentally altered TikTok features including autoplay/infinite-scroll friction. Added friction reduced some compulsive engagement patterns but also reduced user experience and did not automatically create broad agency. Smooth entertainment can be genuinely enjoyable while a lack of stopping points can make disengagement unusually hard. |
| 173 | BlackBerry and constant availability · 2000s | [Academy of Management proceedings](https://journals.aom.org/doi/10.5465/ambpp.2006.27169074) | Research on intensive BlackBerry use found that mobile email could create feelings of control and value while also encouraging compulsive checking and escalating expectations of constant availability. A tool that handles real urgency can cause ordinary messages to inherit urgent status. |
| 174 | U.S. Office of Personnel Management breach · 2015 | [U.S. GAO information-security review](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-17-614) | The OPM breaches compromised highly sensitive background-investigation information affecting millions of people, including about 5.6 million sets of fingerprints. Permanent biometric identifiers carry a different long-tail risk from credentials that can simply be changed after a breach. |
| 175 | Chuck Feeney and “Giving While Living” · 1984–2020 | [Atlantic Philanthropies history](https://www.atlanticphilanthropies.org/wp-content/uploads/2017/05/History_of_the_Giving_While_Living_Ethic.pdf) | Feeney transferred nearly all his personal fortune to his foundation in 1984 and later committed it to spending down during his lifetime. Wealth remained capable of growing, but he deliberately treated accumulated capital as something eventually meant to be used. |
