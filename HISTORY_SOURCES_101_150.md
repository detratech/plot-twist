# Plot Twist Historical Example Source Ledger — IDs 101–150

This is an editorial/research record only. It is **not loaded by the game**.

The same rules used in the earlier source ledgers apply here: accuracy and fit outrank fame, strong institutional/academic/archival sources are preferred, and the game wording is narrowed whenever the familiar version of a story is cleaner than the actual record.

## IDs 101–125

| ID | Example used in game | Research source | What the source supports |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 101 | Volkswagen “Dieselgate” · 2015 | [U.S. EPA: Learn About Volkswagen Violations](https://www.epa.gov/vw/learn-about-volkswagen-violations) | Volkswagen installed software that detected emissions testing and changed vehicle behaviour during the test, while normal-road nitrogen-oxide emissions could be far higher. A technically real test result can still support a false overall impression when the test situation is deliberately manipulated. |
| 102 | Old/fake Hurricane Sandy images · 2012 | [The Guardian contemporaneous #FakeSandy verification page](https://www.theguardian.com/world/us-news-blog/2012/oct/29/fake-hurricane-sandy-photos) | During Sandy, authentic older storm photographs, unrelated dramatic images, and fabricated composites circulated as if they documented the current event. Real media becomes false evidence when date/origin is silently changed. |
| 103 | Denver International Airport baggage system · 1990s | [U.S. GAO testimony](https://www.gao.gov/products/t-rcedaimd-95-184) and [GAO baggage-system fact sheet](https://www.gao.gov/products/rced-95-241fs) | Denver’s opening was delayed repeatedly in part because of the automated baggage system, and only part of the system functioned when the airport opened. Ambitious promises become planning liabilities when technical readiness and calendar commitments diverge. |
| 104 | Andrew Wakefield’s MMR paper · 1998–2011 | [BMJ on the GMC findings](https://www.bmj.com/content/340/bmj.c593) and [BMJ on the Lancet retraction](https://www.bmj.com/content/340/bmj.c696) | The GMC found Wakefield dishonest and irresponsible; the Lancet later retracted the paper after findings that important elements were incorrect. Separate reporting also documented undisclosed legal/financial relationships. A conflict of interest justifies scrutiny, but the scientific claim was ultimately rejected because the evidence and research conduct failed, not merely because a motive existed. |
| 105 | *Tarasoff v. Regents of the University of California* · 1976 | [California Supreme Court opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/17/425.html) | The court held that when a therapist determines, or reasonably should determine, that a patient presents serious danger to another person, reasonable care may require protective action. Confidentiality is important but not absolute where grave identifiable harm is at stake. |
| 106 | Tarasoff duty to protect · 1970s | [California Supreme Court opinion](https://law.justia.com/cases/california/supreme-court/3d/17/425.html) | The opinion explicitly balances confidential therapeutic communication against a protective duty and frames disclosure/action around what is reasonably necessary to avert danger rather than indiscriminate sharing. |
| 107 | Friars Club roast tradition · 20th century | [Los Angeles Times history of the Friars Club](https://www.latimes.com/archives/la-xpm-1996-09-22-ca-46294-story.html) and [LA Times retrospective on roasts](https://www.latimes.com/archives/blogs/show-tracker/story/2010-11-05/updated-friars-club-celebrates-50-years-of-roasts-with-new-dvd-set) | Celebrity roasts were an established opt-in honour built around intentionally harsh/off-colour jokes. A longtime participant recalled that the roastee knew the humour was meant as an honour while some “strained laughter” could nevertheless be painful to watch. Broad consent to the format does not prove every particular barb lands harmlessly. |
| 108 | Ignaz Semmelweis and handwashing · 1840s | [Medical-history review in PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7094481/) | Semmelweis demonstrated dramatically lower puerperal-fever mortality after chlorinated-lime handwashing, faced strong resistance, and later became increasingly combative toward critics. Poor delivery could damage persuasion without making the mortality data false. |
| 109 | “Daisy” political advertisement · 1964 | [Library of Congress: Television and Politics](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/hope-for-america/television-and-politics.html) and [LOC film record](https://www.loc.gov/item/2024601043/) | The Johnson campaign’s famous ad used a child, countdown and nuclear explosion to evoke fear that Goldwater might use nuclear weapons despite never naming him in the spot. Emotional presentation can defeat a frightening implied version of an opponent without carefully arguing the opponent’s strongest actual position. |
| 110 | Cuban Missile Crisis naval quarantine · 1962 | [U.S. Office of the Historian](https://history.state.gov/milestones/1961-1968/cuban-missile-crisis) | Kennedy’s advisers considered options including air strikes and invasion; Kennedy instead announced a naval quarantine while negotiations continued. A consequential third option existed between immediate attack and simply accepting the missiles, but creating it carried real cost and risk. |
| 111 | McCarthy / Army–McCarthy hearings · 1954 | [U.S. Senate Historical Office](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/investigations/mccarthy-and-army-mccarthy-hearings.htm) | The televised hearings exposed McCarthy’s aggressive accusation-heavy questioning and treatment of witnesses. The episode is a strong historical illustration of how an accusation can be embedded in the way a question is framed before the premise has been established. |
| 112 | Pluto reclassification · 2006 | [International Astronomical Union resolution](https://www.iau.org/IAU/Iau/News/PR2006/iau-2006-general-assembly-resolution-votes.aspx) | New Solar-System discoveries pushed astronomers to formalize a general definition of “planet”; the same rule created the dwarf-planet category and changed Pluto’s classification. Legitimate definition revision applies a principled category rule broadly rather than inventing a one-case exception to protect a favourite conclusion. |
| 113 | Doctors in cigarette advertising · 1920s–1950s | [Stanford Research Into the Impact of Tobacco Advertising](https://tobacco.stanford.edu/cigarettes/doctors-smoking/more-doctors-smoke-camels/) and [Smithsonian tobacco-ad collection](https://www.si.edu/media/NMAH/NMAH-AC1224-0000001-000001.pdf) | Tobacco advertisers used physician imagery and survey-like claims such as “More Doctors Smoke Camels” to borrow medical authority. Stanford’s archive documents sampling methods involving free cigarette distribution at conventions. Selected expert-looking endorsements can create a misleading impression of the broader evidence/base rate. |
| 114 | Salem witch trials / spectral evidence · 1692 | [Massachusetts Archives Digital Repository: Salem Witch Trials](https://digitalarchives.sec.state.ma.us/the-trials/) | Spectral evidence relied on claims about invisible apparitions that accused people had little practical ability to disprove. The later court’s refusal to rely on spectral evidence dramatically changed outcomes. A claim structured so the accused cannot meaningfully falsify it shifts the burden of proof unfairly. |
| 115 | Tacoma Narrows Bridge · 1940 | [Federal Highway Administration](https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/bridge/pubs/hif11016/hif11016.pdf) | Severe oscillation was observed soon after opening, and the bridge later collapsed under wind. Those observations revealed behaviour the prevailing design assumptions had not adequately captured and drove greater attention to bridge aerodynamics. |
| 116 | Great Depression · 1929–1930s | [Federal Reserve History overview](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/-/media/Project/FedHistory/FedHistory/Documents/essaysPDFs/Overview_-The-History-of-the-Federal-Reserve-_-Federal-Reserve-History.pdf) and [Banking Panics of 1930–31](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/-/media/Project/FedHistory/FedHistory/Documents/essaysPDFs/Banking-Panics-of-1930-31-_-Federal-Reserve-History.pdf) | The downturn involved the stock crash, banking panics, monetary contraction and other interacting domestic/international forces; economists still debate relative causal weight. A major outcome can have several important causes without one variable explaining the entire event. |
| 117 | Eisenhower’s D-Day decision · 1944 | [U.S. National Archives, “Ok, We’ll Go”](https://www.archives.gov/publications/prologue/2014/spring/d-day.pdf) | Eisenhower chose to launch amid uncertainty and wrote an “in case of failure” note accepting responsibility, explicitly saying the decision had been based on the best information available. Outcome knowledge should not be smuggled backward into what was knowable at decision time. |
| 118 | Concorde and the sunk-cost problem · 1960s–1970s | [UK Parliament research briefing](https://researchbriefings.files.parliament.uk/documents/SN02764/SN02764.pdf) and [academic discussion of the “Concorde fallacy”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21227838/) | Concorde became a namesake for sunk-cost reasoning, but the historical record is more complicated: the UK tried to withdraw in 1964–65 and faced treaty/legal exit costs. Past spending should not justify future spending by itself, while genuine future costs of quitting still belong in the calculation. The audited game wording should preserve this distinction. |
| 119 | Bazalgette’s London sewer system · 1850s–1870s | [Institution of Civil Engineers](https://www.ice.org.uk/what-is-civil-engineering/infrastructure-projects/london-sewer-system) and [London Museum](https://www.londonmuseum.org.uk/collections/london-stories/how-bazalgette-built-londons-first-super-sewer/) | After the Great Stink, Bazalgette led construction of a vast interceptor-sewer and pumping network that redirected waste downstream and transformed sanitation/public health. It did not need to solve every possible city-health problem to be a valuable partial solution. |
| 120 | Connecticut / Great Compromise · 1787 | [U.S. Senate Historical Office](https://www.senate.gov/about/resources/pdf/book-scenes-koed-2023.pdf) | Delegates were deadlocked over representation; the eventual design combined proportional representation in the House with equal state representation in the Senate. The compromise was defended as a structure serving two competing representation concerns, not simply because it landed halfway between two numbers. |
| 121 | Americans with Disabilities Act · 1990 | [ADA.gov statutory/regulatory materials](https://www.ada.gov/law-and-regs/ada/) | The ADA makes reasonable accommodation a core legal concept, including modifications that enable qualified people with disabilities to participate. Treating people fairly can require principled exceptions tied to a rule’s purpose rather than identical treatment in every circumstance. |
| 122 | Higgs boson exclusions before discovery · 2011 | [CERN CMS Physics Analysis Summary](https://cds.cern.ch/record/1370076/files/HIG-11-011-pas.pdf) | CMS non-detections excluded large Higgs mass ranges at specified confidence levels before the later discovery. “Finding nothing” becomes informative when an experiment was capable of detecting what should have appeared in the tested range. |
| 123 | Halley’s Comet prediction · 1758 | [Royal Society](https://royalsociety.org/blog/2023/10/magnetic-halley/) | Edmond Halley predicted the return of the comet from prior observations and gravitational reasoning; it returned after his death as predicted. Accurate foreknowledge/prediction is not the same thing as causing the event predicted. |
| 124 | D-Day weather forecasting · 1944 | [UK Met Office archive](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/research/library-and-archive/archive-hidden-treasures/d-day) and [Met Office D-Day forecast history](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/about-us/who-we-are/our-history/met-office-d-day-weather-forecast) | British and American forecasting teams used different methods and initially produced substantially different forecasts; James Stagg had to synthesize the evidence for a specific place/time decision. Apparent disagreement can shrink or become intelligible once scope, timing and method are specified. |
| 125 | Watergate evidence accumulation · 1972–1974 | [Richard Nixon Presidential Library / National Archives Watergate Gallery](https://www.nixonlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/2018-07/National%20Archives%20Nixon%20Library%20Opens%20New%20Watergate%20Gallery.pdf) and [National Archives Nixon tape holdings](https://www.nixonlibrary.gov/sites/default/files/2019-07/AoGP-ScopeContent.pdf) | Watergate was reconstructed through documents, testimony, investigations, journalism and White House recordings rather than one magical clue. The National Archives preserves Special Prosecution Force tapes used in criminal trials alongside broader records, illustrating how independent evidence types accumulated into the case. |
